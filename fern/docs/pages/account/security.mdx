Your user is the key to every account you belong to — including the organization's balance and its phone lines. Securing it is not optional for anyone with the Owner role, and it's cheap: two-factor authentication takes a minute to set up and is required on every sign-in afterwards.

Everything on this page is **per user**, not per account: it follows you across every account you can access. Find it in the user menu (top right): **Profile**, **Security** and **Sessions**.

## Profile

Your name, email and phone number, and which are verified. Email is verified by a link at sign-up; the phone by a 6-digit SMS code (valid 10 minutes). Both are needed before an account can place calls, and the phone is what receives an SMS notification if you enable one.

Changing your email or phone repeats the verification for the new value. **Connected accounts** shows whether Google and Apple sign-in are linked.

## Signing in

Several ways, and you can have any combination on one user:

- **Email and password.** Change the password from Security; the current password is required to set a new one. A user created through Google or Apple can add a password here to sign in either way.
- **Continue with Google.** Links your Google identity to the user. Google handles the credential; if two-factor authentication is on, Talkif still asks for the code after Google.
- **Continue with Apple.** Signs in with your Apple ID, optionally behind Hide My Email. Two-factor authentication applies the same way. See [Sign in with Apple](/account/passkeys-and-sign-in-with-apple#sign-in-with-apple).
- **Passkey.** Unlocks with Touch ID, Face ID, Windows Hello or the device PIN, and skips the 2FA code because it already proves both the device and you. See [Passkeys](/account/passkeys-and-sign-in-with-apple#passkeys).

Failed sign-ins are rate-limited per address — a handful of quick attempts, then a slow trickle — so a password can't be brute-forced, and you may briefly be locked out after several typos.

## Two-factor authentication

Time-based one-time passwords (TOTP), compatible with Google Authenticator, Authy, 1Password and any standard app.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Enable">
    **User menu → Security → Two-Factor Authentication → Enable.** Scan the QR code with your app and enter the six-digit code it shows.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the backup codes">
    You get **10 single-use backup codes** (8 characters each, shown as `XXXX-XXXX`). They're the only way back in if you lose the phone. Put them in a password manager now — they are not shown again.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sign in with it">
    From then on, every password, Google or Apple sign-in asks for the current code, or a backup code. A passkey sign-in doesn't: the passkey is already a second factor.
  </Step>
</Steps>

To disable, confirm with your current password. If you're an organization Owner, consider it mandatory; you're the only person who can change the organization's billing.

## Sessions

**User menu → Sessions** shows this device's session. **Log out of other devices** ends every session except the current one — the right response to a lost laptop or a suspected compromise. Changing your password does not by itself end other sessions; do both.
