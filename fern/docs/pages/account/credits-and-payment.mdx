Talkif is prepaid: you add money, calls draw it down. This page is the mechanics of adding it and keeping it topped up. How the balance is *spent* — buckets, the $1.00 admission minimum, what happens at zero — is in [Credits and pricing](/concepts/credits-and-pricing); if you haven't read that, the rules below will feel arbitrary.

Everything on this page is done by the account **Owner**. Admins and Members see the balance and the charges, but can't pay.

## Topping up

**Billing → Overview → Add Balance.** In **Add Funds**, pick an amount or type one between **\$10 and \$10,000**; pay with a saved card, or through a hosted checkout if you haven't saved one yet. The balance updates the moment the payment succeeds — no invoice cycle, no waiting.

Purchased balance never expires and is spent last, after any promotional or bonus credit.

Your first payment also lifts the limits that apply to new accounts: free credit can then pay for phone numbers, and the account can hold more of them. See [Buy a number](/telephony/buy-a-number#paying-for-it).

## Payment methods

Cards are handled by Stripe; Talkif stores only a reference and the card's last digits. **Billing → Payment Methods** lets you add cards, label them ("Corporate Visa"), and choose a **default** — the one used for manual top-ups by default and for auto-recharge. 3-D Secure is supported for cards that require it.

## Auto-recharge

The single most useful thing to switch on. When a charge leaves the balance below a **threshold**, Talkif charges your card for a fixed **amount** — so a busy campaign never stalls on an empty balance.

| Setting | Range |
|---|---|
| **Threshold** — recharge when the balance falls below | \$1 – \$1,000 |
| **Add Amount** — how much to add | \$5 – \$500 |
| **Payment Method** | the default card, or a specific one |

Switch it on from **Billing → Overview** (the auto-recharge card); set the threshold, amount and card under **Manage**, in **Billing → Payment Methods → Auto-Recharge**. Pick a threshold above your biggest plausible hour of spend and an amount that covers a day; you can always change both.

<Warning>
  The amount field accepts up to \$1,000, but a recharge above **\$500** is never charged. Keep the amount at \$500 or less.
</Warning>

A recharge starts when a charge — a call, a number's rental — takes the balance below the threshold, not when you switch it on. If the balance is already below the threshold, the next charge triggers it; top up by hand if you need the money now.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="When a recharge fails" defaultOpen>
    A failed recharge is retried **1 day** later, then **3 days** after the next failure, then **5 days** after that — four attempts in all. Later charges can start a new attempt too, at most once an hour and at most **5 times in 24 hours**, so a broken card can't be hammered. If the bank asks for 3-D Secure, auto-recharge can't complete it for you: it stops and you're notified to top up by hand. You get an email when a recharge fails and when it stops trying.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Auto-recharge and suspension">
    While auto-recharge is switched on, the account is **never suspended** for a negative balance — the recharge is expected to cover it. Calls still need \$1.00 of balance to start, so if the card keeps failing, new calls and campaigns are refused until a recharge succeeds or you top up by hand. Keep an eye on the **Auto-recharge failed** email.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## When a manual payment fails

The balance simply doesn't change; nothing is charged twice, and Talkif doesn't retry the payment for you. **Add Funds** shows the reason. Common causes: a card limit, 3-D Secure not completed, a bank blocking an unfamiliar merchant. Try again with the same card after the bank clears it, or use another.

## Disputes

If you dispute a charge with your bank, the disputed amount plus a **$15 processing fee** is held from your balance while the dispute is open. If it resolves in your favour the hold is released; if not, it's deducted. Contact support before disputing — a billing error is fixed far faster as an adjustment.
